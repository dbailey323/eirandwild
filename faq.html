Frequently asked questions

Everything you need to know

Clear answers to common questions about exercising safely in pregnancy, postnatal recovery, bringing your baby, baby massage and doula care.

Pregnancy & antenatal fitness

Is it safe for me to exercise during pregnancy?

Both the Royal College of Obstetricians and Gynaecologists (RCOG) and the American College of Sports Medicine (ACSM) list numerous benefits of physical activity during pregnancy compared to staying sedentary. I am a fully qualified pre and postnatal fitness specialist, trained to adapt exercises safely for each trimester.

What are the benefits of exercising when pregnant?

Regular exercise during pregnancy can:

  • Maintain cardiovascular fitness and muscle tone
  • Lower the risk of high blood pressure and gestational diabetes
  • Support healthy maternal weight gain
  • Ease constipation, backache and pelvic discomfort
  • Improve mood, self-image and sleep
  • Build stamina and endurance for labour and birth
Should I tell you as soon as I know I'm pregnant?

You can tell me whenever feels comfortable to you! If you share early on, rest assured this stays completely confidential. The earlier I know, the sooner I can adapt movements and offer trimester-safe options.

Should I speak to my midwife or GP before starting?

Yes. You should always speak with your midwife, GP or obstetrician before starting exercise. They hold your detailed medical history and can advise on any individual precautions.

Can I eat extra during pregnancy?

Although the old saying claims you are "eating for two", this isn't medically accurate. In your third trimester your body needs approximately 200 extra calories a day (and around 500 extra while breastfeeding). This supports healthy maternal energy and baby's growth.

I haven't exercised before pregnancy. Can I still attend?

Yes! We build up gradually with gentle progressions. Pregnancy is a time for maintaining fitness and building functional strength rather than peak intensity. If you are new to fitness, gentle movement will help with stamina for birth and postnatal recovery.

Postnatal recovery & little ones

When can I start exercising again after birth?

A minimum of 6 weeks after a vaginal birth, or a minimum of 12 weeks after a caesarean. You should always get clearance from your GP or healthcare professional at your 6–8 week postnatal check before returning to structured workouts.

I'm breastfeeding. Can I still exercise?

Yes! Exercises are adapted to avoid breast discomfort or excess pressure. If baby needs to feed mid-class, feed away! There is zero judgement in our classes, and feeding and soothing babies is completely normal.

Can I bring my baby to the classes?

Yes! That is the heart of Eir & Wild. Pre-crawlers can relax on a mat or cosy blanket right next to you. If your little one is on the move, they can relax in a pushchair or carrier while you enjoy your workout without chasing them around!

Will we check for diastasis recti (abdominal separation)?

Yes. We do a gentle abdominal check at the start of your course, and exercises are specifically chosen to safely re-knit and strengthen your deep core and abdominals after birth.

Baby massage & doula support

When can I attend baby massage?

Babies are welcome from 6–8 weeks old up until pre-crawling. All the techniques and organic oils used are gentle on delicate infant skin.

Are doulas only for home births?

No! Doulas provide non-judgemental support across all birth settings: home births, birth centres, hospital delivery suites, and planned or unplanned caesarean births. Postnatal doula care takes place in your own home to nurture your recovery.
